# Research Craft Principles

- Lead with the claim or conclusion, then evidence; replace vague hedging with explicit assumptions.
- Document why a method fits the evidence and constraints, not merely what method ran.
- State every assumption. Mark each claim as evidenced or inferred, and prefer falsifiable claims; expose any condition whose failure would invalidate a conclusion.
- Give each paragraph or section one claim; split compound units so each contribution can be traced, cited, and challenged.
- Declare scope limits and known limitations before the analysis, not defensively afterward.
- When a counterexample needs a carve-out, revise the general theory to include it; multiple carve-outs are multiple hypotheses.
- Narrow the research question until available evidence and methods can answer it fully. “Future work” is valid only for scope never claimed, not an unanswered part of the original question.
